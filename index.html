Guide to Working Safely in the Heat

Stay informed about heat risks, symptoms and what to do if you or a coworker gets sick on the job.

Emergency

Heat stroke = call 911

Call 911 now

Call 911 right away if the person:

  • Stops sweating
  • Seems confused, can't talk clearly or passes out
  • Has a seizure
  • Has very hot, red or dry skin

While you wait for help

  • Move the person to a cool place and remove outer clothing
  • Cool the body fast with cold, wet cloths, ice packs or cool water on the head, neck, armpits and groin
  • Stay with them until emergency medical services arrive

Know the signs of heat illness

Early warning signs

  • Heavy sweating
  • Headache, dizziness or confusion
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Muscle cramps
  • Weakness or feeling faint

Take action right away

  • Move to shade or a cooler indoor spot
  • Sip cool water (small, frequent sips)
  • For heat cramps, have a snack or drink that replaces carbs and electrolytes every 15 to 20 minutes
  • Loosen clothing; use cool cloths or ice packs on the neck, armpits or groin
  • Rest until you feel better — don't go back to heavy work right away
  • Take a worker with heat exhaustion to a clinic or emergency room, or call 911 if medical care is unavailable

Source: The National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH)

Report unsafe conditions

You have the right to a safe workplace and to report hazards without retaliation. Michigan has no specific heat rule, but MIOSHA expects employers to take steps to prevent heat illness — such as providing water, rest and shade — and it investigates complaints.
